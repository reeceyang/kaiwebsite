Twice alive, Portrait of an angel, Sea wall

June 6, 2026 
Sea wall

That darkness tugged down shore from sand by tide
retreating in another cycle’s trough
was clawed by frothing waves to the inside
of sludge-browned surface seabirds skidded off
to duck for fish in stinking delta’s flow.
The sand, now lightened, melted so I saw
a Portuguese armada’s clear blue glow
emerge from divots to be eaten raw.
Capricious memory; averted fall;
inevitable darkness-dampened legs
reached up again succeeding sea’s white wall
and left me barrelling inside the dregs
of liquid glass which over me would break:
no wall, then, between glass and sea opaque.
Portrait of an angel

The morning’s shifting light through your blinds drew
a portrait of a sleeping angel, here
and there just strung with lines of lustrous dew
like ropes around a body, taut.            
                                                             My fear
would hold us, as the setting sun might pin
our shadows out of reach, in perfect place
to watch as falser wings were torn from skin
outside the room we’d earned by heaven’s grace.

And if your love was shaped by mortal hands?
If palms apart from mine had cause to jut
into your holy sides, sweat-rivered lands?

To forget’s to purify. 	              And what
if heaven’s fire blackened, curled, and fell
silent	                       
                                 as nights fall over hell?
Twice alive

You pour me tea. An amber circle lights
from the inside. Fevered, honeyed-over,
slowed by nausea, I tip back, your thumb bites
at my chin. As russet eyes close slower
than flies can tread across an iris. So 
tawny wings spread silent, cut an owl’s
shape in light. As turtle shells sound hollow
when kicked, so the matted coyote howls
and its call, splintered between trees, finds me
frocked in lichen. Fungal, these reaching hands
and leaves are, bunching over rot-rimmed tea-
cup, so unfocused eyes tease out gold bands
so I looked up through the branches, radiating
so you taxidermied the end of spring.

Notes

I wrote sonnets just to try writing sonnets. Clearly though I got tired of the exercise after literally two sonnets.

“Portrait of an angel” was inspired by two lines in the Forrest Gander poem “Into the Mountains”: You must forget the other hands that have opened her robe and If the orange lichen—gossiping across boulders—blackens, curls, and goes silent?

The title “Twice alive” and the general idea of lichen came from the Forrest Gander collection, Twice Alive. The actual poem is not directly inspired though. In general I like the thread running through the collection: casting one’s life beyond itself, forming a superorganism in interaction with a life outside one’s own.
