Glass cathedrals & Tonight it’s 1990

April 14, 2026

The past year has brought me beauty and clarity. One of its greater gifts was introducing me to poetry. It’s a thrilling feeling to start reading poetry at this time in my life, like I’ve just stumbled upon this rich, latent dimension to the universe that so many others have already tread and mapped out before me. I’ve been reading: collections by Arthur Sze; collections by Forrest Gander; Nabokov’s Pale Fire, but just the poem, over and over; and (Fitzgerald’s translation of) Homer’s Odyssey.

So here are two poems. “Glass cathedrals” is about a night this past fall in which I woke up very slowly and, in the space between slumber and full awareness, traveled through a land I had never seen before. “Tonight it’s 1990” is about tripping for the first time next to my (sober) friend, who found the experience really funny. It was going to be a sonnet but I couldn’t think of a closing couplet, but that’s okay since we live in 2026 and no one writes sonnets anymore anyway.

Glass cathedrals

Tonight the moon rises above
	a city of glass cathedrals.
The lowest branch of a cherry tree bows
	under the weight of a lantern.
A stag’s head whips around
	the bend of a road shouldered in black.

I wake over hours inside the moonlight
	and find no shadows cast
by the thicket growing from
	where your body meets the world.
You ask the question a different way
	each time. My answer stays the same—

Though I know, once it’s washed flat
	by headlights rounding the corner,
or held up against the burnished point
	of a spire, or dappled in lantern glow,
it’ll peel off, flake away, faster
	than my own whisper into the wind.

For in that city there is no metaphor,
	no meaning nor witness under a fair moon.
For hours I don’t dare to stretch my limbs
	or relax my ribs under bramble’s clutch,
as if even I don’t believe the percussive
	wish—I insisted—in hot breath—

for the instant just before
	dawn sets stained glass aflare
		kerosene wets the forest floor
			antlers pierce a windshield—

when daybreak librates at the skyline and
	petals edge with a burgeoning wildfire’s yellow
		and a dashboard mirrors roadkill while
			overhead swells an event horizon—
		
the instant just after my abdomen 
	has twisted—quadriceps fired—
		after I’ve jerked towards the light
			and can only watch the fall.
Tonight it’s 1990

Tonight it’s 1990. I’m nineteen
in Boulder, wearing what I’m wearing now,
telling you about my weed guy. He’s seen
it all, I’m telling you, you laugh at how
stupid I get when I’m into someone.
I keep thinking I should leave you alone
to sleep, but this loop I’ve done and undone
over decades, and still it cuts to bone
every time I make you laugh—cuts deeper,
I mean, than the year I’m in, or if my
weed guy loves me, or what kind of creature
I am, hearing damp snow, tasting lit sky.
