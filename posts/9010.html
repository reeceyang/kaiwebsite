In the past few months, I moved to the Bay. I got my driver’s license. I started dating someone. And I finished up the work I’ve been doing for the past year.

While I was working, I felt stupid, confused, and incompetent 90% of the time, and excited 10% of the time. Now, I’m so glad I finished, and I’m proud of the final product. It’s the first technical project I’ve ever carried all the way from my own vision to the code + dataset + model release.

I was scared I might not ever do it—that due to some internal defect, I would never be able to do good work, that this didn’t depend on project or environment but purely on my own innate shortcomings.

But it takes a long time to make something you’re proud of. In the past year as I’ve tried hard to build the practices that I’ll be happy to carry into the future, I felt increasingly demoralized whenever something went wrong or I pushed back a deadline.

I’ve been writing the same short story since January. So many times, I felt so frustrated, I wanted to just call it done. Sometimes, that frees me; but often I discover more in the next cycle of rewriting and editing.

I want to be more patient. It’s important to have standards and to be honest with myself, but in the end, I want to treat my ideas and my work with love and not with contention.
